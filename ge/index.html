Welcoming the World With Industrial Vision

GE is one of the Olympic Committee’s top global sponsors, but Rio wasn’t the usual consumer play. The Games put GE in a room with the government, industry, and sector leaders who actually procure and regulate its technology, deliberately. GE wanted to use that room to build relationships with them directly, not just visibility with the public.

We built the platform for that at one of GE’s largest industrial sites in Brazil: a live experience combining interactive content, VR, game design, and generative art, designed to make GE’s most invisible technologies, the systems running global manufacturing and infrastructure, the heroes of its own story.

Guests flew through Rio’s own water management system. They stepped inside a VR gallery of GE’s infrastructure-scale product portfolio, seeing the configuration and systems integration of equipment that’s normally sealed, in use, or scaled past human reach, the kind of access no working plant or site can offer. You can’t crack open a running turbine for a government minister. VR could. They ran power generation simulations and worked through interactive content on how GE uses big data to predict and prevent industrial outages before they happen.

One team environment put guests in charge of balancing power distribution across tidal, gas turbine, wind, and solar generation for a simulated Rio de Janeiro, a real systems problem dressed as a game. The other was a tandem kayak sprint, teams racing head to head, Olympic spirit turned into an actual contest in the room. One sharpened thinking. The other just got people’s blood up. Both got a room of competitors and regulators working the same side, for once.

The technology GE runs on is built to disappear. Pipelines, turbines, grids, water systems, they’re only noticed when they fail. For 7 days in Rio, we made that invisible infrastructure the main event, and put the people who decide its future inside it, racing each other in kayaks between sessions.
